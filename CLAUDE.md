# ProtoProsthetic (ProtoMotions fork)

RL co-learning of a simulated human with a right transtibial amputation and a powered ankle
prosthesis (OSL ankle, AK80-9 motor with a bench-identified motor model), in IsaacLab. The human
imitates walking motions (mimic env); the prosthesis has its own policy and sensors.

Two experiment stages (examples/experiments/mimic/):
- `mlp_marl_magic.py`: trains the HUMAN only. The human policy also drives the prosthesis motor
  angle with fixed Kp/Kd ("magic" prosthesis: `prosthetic_magic_index`).
- `mlp_marl_pre_trained.py`: loads a magic-trained human (`--load-pretrained-humanoid --checkpoint
  results/<magic run>/humanoid_last.ckpt`) and co-learns a separate PROSTHESIS policy
  (`CoLearningOrchestrator`, protomotions/agents/multi_agent_orchestrator/).
The human and prosthesis networks must see the same inputs in both files (in_keys order matters).

## Socket / residual-limb model (smpl10bis_tissue)

Why it changed: in `smpl10bis` the skin<->socket interface was 8 compliant contacts between links
of the same articulation. PhysX converges very slowly on that self-contact: at 360 Hz / 32
iterations the socket pistoned ~5x more under load than the converged answer, i.e. the training
pistoning was solver error. Joint drives are solved exactly, rigid contacts converge fast, so:
- each skin patch (`skin_box_<region>_<top|bottom>`) is on a prismatic joint `<patch>_tissue`
  along its local z (= socket plate normal, + = compressed); drive = 4 x the original skin material
  stiffness per region (posterior 46666, medial 74500, lateral 104000, anterior 140000 N/m: ratios
  1 : 1.60 : 2.23 : 3.00 must be kept), damping 4 x 2000 for all; contact rigid + frictionless
- suspension (liner/suction/sleeve) = `suspension_slide` drive 27000 N/m, 1000 Ns/m, rest target
  +0.022 m (+ = socket away from the limb; carries the ~3 kg prosthesis, tissue unloaded at rest);
  `suspension_x/y/z` 350 Nm/rad, 175 Nms/rad (y = limb long axis = torsion)
- socket plates extended 5 cm distally (box corners caught plate edges and locked the socket);
  not proximally (would hit the thigh earlier in knee flexion)
- no R_Knee (tibia) <-> socket collision (no rigid end stop); pylon shortened 22 mm for standing
  parity (prosthetic side carries 52 % of body weight standing)
- calibrated against static pistoning data (Gholizadeh 2011/2012: ~10 mm loading->unloading,
  pull-out ~4/6/8 mm at 30/60/90 N pin lock, ~70 % less with suction; RSA case study: stiffening).
  Model: ~15 mm unloaded->800 N, 3.2 mm at 90 N pull-out. Rotations are NOT calibrated (no data).
- the slide target per robot comes from `RobotConfig.passive_dof_targets`; `smpl10bis` and
  `_merged` keep -0.075
- physics default 270 Hz / decimation 3 (90 Hz control) / 16 pos + 1 vel iterations: ~0.5 mm RMS
  pistoning error vs a 1440 Hz/128 reference. Avoid 180 Hz: the motor model's 3 ms torque lag is
  shorter than the step. Velocity iterations barely matter; position iterations do.

## How DOFs/bodies are selected (names, not indices)

- Passive DOFs (`passive_dof_names` in the experiment + every `*_tissue`): targets overwritten every
  physics step (`IsaacLabSimulator._write_passive_dof_defaults`), excluded from human actions.
- Human action set: every DOF except `suspension*`, `*_tissue`, `R_Ankle_y`, `Motor`, `R_Toe_y`
  (63 DOFs). `Motor` is torque-driven via the motor model (BUILT_IN_PD_HYBRID).
- Human blind obs (`base_agent/agent.py`): bodies with prosthetic/skin/socket/osl/r_ankle in the
  name are removed; contact forces keep the skin patches. `skin_patch_obs` (HumanoidObs, only if
  the robot has `_tissue` joints) = per patch deformation (mm) + normal force (x100 N); fed to the
  human actor and both critics, not the prosthesis actor. Critics use full `max_coords_obs`, so
  changing the number of bodies changes critic input size (old critic weights won't load).
- Prosthesis sensors: `ProstheticObs` samples every physics step through `simulator.sensor_tap`
  (latency model in `sensor_model.py`), reading only shank (`prosthetic_assembly2`) and foot
  (`R_Ankle`) bodies via `get_body_dof_subset`.

## Performance notes

Already done (bit-identical results, verified with `bench_sim.py --trace`): one actuator group per
parameter set instead of one per DOF (was ~1000 kernel launches per physics step), one contact
sensor for all contact bodies without the unused filter, lean per-step sensor reads, no GPU->CPU
sync in the sensor history. Biggest remaining costs per control step: PhysX, then env
post-physics (obs + rewards, ~25 ms at 2048 envs). Training at 2048 envs: smpl10bis_merged
360 Hz/32 ~11.8k env-steps/s; smpl10bis_tissue 270 Hz/16 ~16.7k env-steps/s.

## Gotchas

- Check `nvidia-smi` before launching anything on the GPU: a training run is often going.
  Several Isaac processes at once can run out of GPU memory.
- The shell is zsh: `$VAR` holding several arguments is NOT word-split. Use `bash -c '...'`.
- Every training run ends with `AssertionError: Timer Main Timer not started yet!` when it reaches
  `--training-max-steps` (orchestrator end-of-fit bug, harmless).
- Old run dirs (e.g. results/test_pretrained_11) predate config fields like `history_truncation`:
  current `inference_agent.py` cannot rebuild their model (bench_sim.py fills missing defaults).
- Isaac/Kit scripts should `os._exit` after flushing stdout; Kit shutdown is slow and can hang.
- Results of physics changes: compare against a high-iteration / high-rate reference, not against
  the previous low-iteration run (low-iteration contact results can be mostly solver error).
